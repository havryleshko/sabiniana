# What hurts a UK dental practice every month (researched 2026-10-03)

Legend: **[V]** verified from the linked source today. **[Vendor]** a claim by a company selling the fix, so weak evidence. **[I]** my inference.

**Honest limit up front:** I found no survey that asks owners to rank their pains, and no measured figures for hours spent on money admin. Almost everything below comes from accountants' and software vendors' blogs. Treat the ranking as a set of hypotheses that your public free offer will test.

## The pains, as a practice owner would say them

### 1. "My costs keep rising and I can't see which bills crept up"
- BDA figures cited in one article: lab fees up 16.5%, staff up 15%, utilities up 10%, against a 4% NHS contract uplift. [V, [Patient Plan Direct](https://patientplandirect.com/news/rising-expenses-are-squeezing-dental-practices/)]
- NHS England's contribution to dental services is down about 16% in real terms since 2014/15. [V, [House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9597/)]
- Accountants name "lab costs rising faster than income" and "unreconciled supplier payments" (a lump sum with no invoice behind it) as common problems. [V, [Samera](https://samera.co.uk/learning-center/bookkeeping-tips-for-dentists/)]
- **Monthly form:** a new invoice arrives, nobody compares it with last month or with a fair price.
- **Can we solve it?** Yes. This is the cost watch. Rule-based, no regulated work. Biggest lines: lab (about 6.6% of income) and consumables (about 7.5%) in private practices. [V, NHS Digital, see dental-procurement-biggest-version.md]

### 2. "Contracts renew or roll over without me noticing"
- UK card providers must now send end-of-contract trigger messages, and terminal leases for new contracts are capped at 18 months. [V, PSR remedies, see procurement-leakage-market.md]
- Energy: rollover (out-of-contract) rates and broker-sold contracts are a known problem; I only saw article titles, not figures. [I]
- Service contracts (compressor, autoclave, X-ray) are many small monthly plans that renew. [V, [The Probe](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/06/annual-service-contracts-from-as-little-as-45-00-vat-per-month/)]
- **Monthly form:** a renewal date passes and the price quietly goes up.
- **Can we solve it?** Yes. A renewal calendar is the cheapest, most useful part of the monthly page.

### 3. "Month-end money admin is a mess"
- Bank payments with no invoice behind them; NHS, private, hygiene and cosmetic income not kept apart; NHS clawback treated wrongly; books reviewed once a year instead of monthly. [V, Samera]
- NHS payments arrive after superannuation and charge deductions; card settlements, deposits and patient finance each need separate tracking. [V, [Cruse Burke](https://cruseburke.co.uk/bookkeeping-for-dentists/)]
- **Can we solve it?** Partly, but this is accountants' ground (bookkeeping). It probably needs HMRC money-laundering registration. [I, see dental-services.md] Not the wedge.

### 4. "Working out associate pay at month end is a nightmare"
- Splits across NHS and private income, minus lab costs and superannuation. "Calculating these figures at the end of the month can become a nightmare." [V, Cruse Burke]
- Lab and equipment arrangements are a frequent source of disputes. [V, BDA, see dental-services.md]
- **Can we solve it?** Technically yes, but same accountant-territory and AML question as #3. Later.

### 5. "Cash goes out on a schedule, but patients pay late"
- Staff, lab, consumables and rent arrive on fixed dates; payment for long treatments (implants, orthodontics) and third-party patient finance arrives later. Two practices with the same income can be valued very differently depending on how fast they collect. [V, [Finance Derivative](https://www.financederivative.com/why-britains-dental-boom-isnt-translating-into-stronger-cash-flow/), quoting dental accountants A4G]
- **Can we solve it?** Not with cost checks. Adjacent to billing and collections.

### 6. "Patients don't pay, and plan payments fail"
- "Almost every dental practice in the UK has suffered from unpaid dental fees at some point." No numbers given. [V, [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2021/04/09/expert-tips-to-avoid-non-payment-of-dental-treatment-fees/)]
- Failed plan payments: a software vendor claims practices lose 6-12% of collectible revenue before billing sees it. Source is vendor research, region not stated, so I would not rely on it. [Vendor, [mConsent](https://mconsent.net/blog/failed-payment-plans-impact-dental-practice/)]
- Admin problems named: spreadsheets out of date, declined direct debits, arrears building silently, billing and diary systems that don't talk. No numbers. [Vendor, [Lloyd Whyte Flexiplan](https://lloydwhyteflexiplan.co.uk/dental-payment-plan-administration-challenges/)]
- **Can we solve it?** This is the "income side" billing idea. It needs patient health-adjacent data and debt-collection rules. Parked earlier for those reasons. [I]

### 7. Not ours to solve (largest costs and risks)
Staff costs (40-50% of turnover by one source, 15% rise), recruitment, NHS contract delivery and clawback, tax and compliance. These are judgement-heavy or regulated, so they stay out. [I]

## Ranking for us
| Rank | Pain | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost creep (#1) | Biggest unserved money pain, rule-based, unregulated |
| 2 | Renewals (#2) | Cheapest to deliver, clear monthly trigger |
| 3 | Month-end admin (#3) and associate pay (#4) | Real pain, but accountant territory and probable AML registration |
| 4 | Cash timing (#5), unpaid fees (#6) | Real, but income-side and data-heavy |

## What the monthly page would answer
1. **What did I pay this month, and what changed from last month?**
2. **Which bills are above a fair price, and by how much a year?** (illustrative)
3. **What renews in the next 90 days?**
4. **What exactly should I ask each supplier for?**

## What a practice would send each month
Card statement or summary box, lab invoices, supplier invoices, energy and phone bills, a list of contracts with renewal dates. That is about 5-10 documents, mostly PDFs. [I]

## Who decides
The principal dentist (owner) or the practice manager. One decision-maker, so the sale is short. [I]
