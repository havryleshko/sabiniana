# Dental practices: the biggest procurement version (researched 2026-10-03)

Legend: **[V]** verified from the linked source today. **[I]** my inference or arithmetic. **[Vendor]** a claim by someone selling the thing, so not independent proof.

## Bottom line
The biggest version is not "card fees". It is a **monthly cost watch that owns every non-staff bill a practice pays**: lab, consumables, premises and energy, card fees, software, service contracts, equipment finance and insurance. Card fees are just the free front door. The honest catch is that the saving per practice is modest (a few £k a year), so the business only gets big if it reaches thousands of practices, or if it expands into adjacent spend. [I]

## 1. Where a practice's money goes
Typical **mixed** practice: about £926k revenue and 4.1 surgeries. [V, [Dental Elite benchmarking](https://dentalelite.co.uk/articles/benchmarking-survey-a-closer-look-at-the-numbers/)]

Cost lines as % of income, **private practices in England** (NASDAL data, 2019/20): lab 6.6%, materials 7.5%, premises 4.6%, non-clinical staff 18.5%. [V, [NHS Digital](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/publications/statistical/dental-earnings-and-expenses-estimates/2019-20/further-expenses-analysis-ews)] NHS-heavy practices: lab 5.7%, materials 6.2%, premises 4.2%. [V]

Other sources disagree on lab size: one accountancy firm says lab fees alone are 8-15% of turnover [V, [Dental Finance Partners](https://www.dentalfinancepartners.co.uk/blog/practice-accounting/laboratory-costs-dental-accounts-uk)]; another says lab and materials together are 8-15%, staff 40-50%, utilities/insurance/software/indemnity 3-5%, marketing 3-8%. [V, [Updent](https://updent.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-a-dental-practice-owner-earn/)] I'd trust the NHS Digital figures most, since they come from practice accounts.

Costs are rising faster than income: BDA figures cited by one source show lab fees up 16.5%, staff up 15% and utilities up 10%, against a 4% NHS contract uplift. [V, [Patient Plan Direct](https://patientplandirect.com/news/rising-expenses-are-squeezing-dental-practices/)]

## 2. What can be attacked (staff is the biggest line but is not procurement)
| Spend line | Rough size on a £926k practice [I] | Why it leaks | Who sells it already |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lab work | ~£61k (6.6%) | Per-unit prices, premium materials on every case, remakes from poor impressions, VAT on unregistered labs [V, Dental Finance Partners] | Nobody sells an independent price check. Buying groups don't cover lab prices [I] |
| Consumables/materials | ~£69k (7.5%) | Pre-negotiated group prices exist | Buying groups: Samera says 6-10% saving on consumables and equipment, free to join, paid by supplier arrangements [Vendor, [Samera](https://samera.co.uk/service/buying-group/)] |
| Premises, energy, utilities | ~£43k (4.6%) | Auto-renewal and rollover rates, broker-sold contracts | Energy brokers, and claims firms: dentalenergy.co.uk takes mis-sold energy claims on no-win-no-fee, solicitor fees capped at 30% [Vendor, [site](https://dentalenergy.co.uk/)] |
| Card fees | ~£1k-£9k (depends on private card sales; see samples) | Flat rates, bands, junk fees | Brokers paid by the processor |
| Service contracts | compressor from £45/month, autoclave from £50, chair/X-ray from £56 + VAT [V, [The Probe](https://the-probe.co.uk/blog/2026/06/annual-service-contracts-from-as-little-as-45-00-vat-per-month/)] | Many small renewals nobody reviews | Equipment suppliers |

## 3. Sizing, honestly
- Sequoia's rule of thumb is that contract leakage is 2-5% of spend (their figure, unsourced in the article). [V, Sequoia, see procurement-leakage-market.md]
- Lab + materials + premises is about £173k on the typical practice. At 2-5% that is **about £3.5k-£8.7k a year** per practice. [I, my arithmetic on assumed leakage]
- Add card fees and energy and it may reach £5k-£15k for a typical practice, but I have **no measured dental savings rates**, only vendor claims.
- Lab is the largest and least served line, and it is also the hardest, because the right lab depends on clinical quality, not only price. [I]
- Market size: I could not verify the number of UK dental practices (the IBISWorld page blocked reads). NHS England put about £2.3bn into dental services in 2024/25. [V, [House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9597/)] The GDC register passed 47,000 dentists in January 2026. [V, headline only, [GDC](https://www.gdc-uk.org/news-blogs/news/detail/2026/01/19/gdc-register-increases-to-over-47-000-dentists)]

## 4. Who has no procurement help
Corporate dental groups have their own buyers [I]. Independent practices rely on a principal and a practice manager, and on free buying groups that are paid by the suppliers they steer you to. So the gap is an **independent, no-commission monthly review across all bills**. [I]

## 5. What the "finished outcome" would be
Each month: the practice sends bills (card statement, lab invoices, supplier invoices, energy, contracts). Alex sends back one page: what changed, what is overpriced, what renews soon, and the exact message to send (or he sends it with a signed letter of authority). That is ongoing work across many bills, not a one-off report. [I]

## 6. Lines not to cross [I, not legal advice]
- No fee-charging claims against suppliers or processors (claims management is FCA-regulated). The energy-claims firms above use solicitors for this.
- No commission from any supplier you recommend. It destroys the independence edge.
- Equipment finance: arranging credit may need FCA permission, so keep to "here is what the rate looks like" until checked.
- Check HMRC AML scope before charging for anything (see dental-services.md).

## 7. Verdict
- **Biggest single line to go after: lab work**, then consumables. It is large, rising and unserved, but hard to judge on price alone.
- **Easiest to start: card fees, service contracts and energy renewals**, which are rule-based and fit the free-outcome stage.
- Not yet verified: dental-specific savings rates, practice count, software costs. A first step to verify cheaply: ask 3-5 practices for lab invoices and compare.

## Update 2026-10-03: do dental accountants already cover this? (public sources only, nobody contacted)
Checked service lists of [Hazlewoods](https://www.hazlewoods.co.uk/expertise/by-sector/dental/), [Page Kirk](https://www.pagekirk.co.uk/dentist/), [Accountants for Dentists](https://www.accountantsfordentists.co.uk/) and consultancy [Hive Business](https://hivebusiness.co.uk/insights/how-to-reduce-costs-in-your-dental-practice). [V]
- All three accountants sell accounts, tax, payroll, NHS pension work, buying/selling practices and **benchmarking** (comparing your staff, lab and material costs with similar practices; Page Kirk uses the NASDAL survey). [V]
- **None advertises supplier, lab or card price negotiation as a service.** Hive advises "renegotiating supplier agreements" but gives no savings figures. [V]
- Reading: accountants *tell you* your lab cost is high versus peers once a year; nobody is paid to *fix* it each month. Benchmarking is the closest competitor, so the offer must be "we act on it every month", not "we compare". [I]
- Accountants also offer "funding support with lenders" (Accountants for Dentists), so equipment finance is not an open gap. [V]
- Lab price spread evidence is US only (offshore vs local crowns, labs "move 10-15%"). No UK numbers found. [V, [US source](https://www.dentalpnl.com/blog/dental-lab-fees.html)]
