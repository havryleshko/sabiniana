## 0. How many practices? (your figure: about 3,300-3,800 private-only plus thousands of mixed)
**Updated 2026-10-03 (second check).**
- **England has 10,160 dental practices (March 2026, Nuffield Trust analysis of the CQC Care Directory and NHSBSA contractor files, published 24 Aug 2026).** 55.6% offer NHS general dentistry, down from 64.6% in 2017. At least 600 practices left NHS general contracts since 2017, and practices without a general NHS contract grew by over 1,000. [V, [Nuffield Trust](https://www.nuffieldtrust.org.uk/news-item/the-great-extraction-the-changing-state-of-nhs-general-dental-services-in-england)]
- So about **4,500 practices in England have no general NHS contract** (44.4% of 10,160). That group is mostly private, but it also includes specialist practices such as NHS orthodontics, so private-only is somewhat lower. [I, my arithmetic]
- **Your 3,300-3,800 private-only figure is plausible for England alone, and probably low for the UK.** I still could not find its source. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are not in the England count. [I]
- Rare Monitor (April 2024, 8,830 UK practices analysed): 80% offer private treatment, 86% are single-site. [V, [Rare Monitor](https://raremonitor.com/blog/in-numbers-uk-private-dental-market/)]
- GDC 2024 survey of **dentists** (not practices): 19% fully private, 14% mostly private, 27% mostly NHS, 15% fully NHS. [V, [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/03/13/only-15-dental-practices-are-fully-nhs-data-shows/)]
- **What matters for us:** about 80% of practices do some private work (Rare Monitor) and all pay the same kinds of bills. The reachable market is a large share of England's roughly 10,000 practices, most of them single-site. [I] (Corrected after re-check: earlier wording said "nearly every practice", which overstated the 80% figure.)

## 1. How much real money does a practice lose each month, by category?
**Honest answer: nobody has measured it. Only the price spread for lab work and the energy premium rest on real numbers.**

| Category | Best real evidence | Rough size per practice per year |
|---|---|---|
| Lab | UK public price lists differ a lot for similar-sounding items (see section 3) [V]. UK negotiation savings not found; a US source says labs "move 10-15%" [V, US only] | On a £61k lab bill, 10-15% is about £6k-£9k [I, uses US %] |
| Energy | Out-of-contract electricity averages 38p/kWh against 26p for competitive fixed deals, a 46% premium. Source sells energy switching [Vendor, [MoneySuperMarket](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/gas-and-electricity/business-energy/a/deemed-and-out-of-contract-rates/)]. No price cap on business energy [V, same page] | Utilities run £400-£900 a month [V, [Updent](https://updent.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-dental-practice-uk/)]. **If** a practice is out of contract, that is about £1.5k-£3.4k a year [I]. Zero if it is on a fixed deal |
| Card fees | Regulator says card acquiring "does not work well" for small merchants, many "do not regularly (if ever) search for providers"; no savings figure given [V, [DLA Piper summary](https://www.dlapiperintelligence.com/investmentrules/blog/articles/2021/payment-systems-regulator-final-report-card-acquiring-services.html)]. A US firm claims 28% average cost cuts [Vendor] | Our fake samples show £1.2k-£6k a year, but they are invented, so not evidence [I] |
| Consumables | A free UK buying group claims 6-10% savings [Vendor, [Samera](https://samera.co.uk/service/buying-group/)] | On about £69k, 6-10% is £4k-£7k, but the group is paid by suppliers [I] |
| Service contracts | Plans start at £45-£87 a month each [V, The Probe] | Under £1k [I] |

**Bottom line:** the realistic range is a few £k to perhaps £15k a year for a typical practice, with lab and consumables the biggest. The wide range is the honest uncertainty. A "found-money" claim should always say "illustrative". [I]

## 2. Do owners feel pains #1 and #2 strongly enough to send documents?
**Desk research cannot answer this.** What I found:
- **For:** the regulator found small merchants rarely shop around, so the problem exists but isn't felt. [V] Free statement-review offers are common (BoonPay, Unique Payments), which suggests a market that responds, though not how many say yes. [V, see ai-native-service-websites.md] A property firm's "send one thing, get a free analysis" form took leads from 1-3 a month to 10+. [V, side-launch-playbooks.md]
- **Against or unknown:** no survey asks practice owners whether they feel cost creep or renewals. Pain that is not felt does not make people send documents. The cost of trust (sending financial documents to a stranger) is real. [I]
- **What lowers the barrier:** let them black out account numbers, no account or call needed, a clear promise on deleting the file. [I]
- **The test:** the public free offer. Success measure for Stage 0: how many practices send a statement within the first weeks of posting. Nobody is asked anything.

## 3. UK lab price spread
Three UK labs that publish prices to dentists (per unit, £). **These are not like-for-like**: materials, services, guarantees and customer tier differ, so read it as a spread, not a ranking.

| Item | Impulse Dental Lab (Glasgow phone code) | The Teeth People | Elevate Dental Designs (Belfast) |
|---|---|---|---|
| e.max crown | £90 | £85 | £145 |
| Zirconia crown | £110 (Katana); £50 (full-contour ZirGlaze) | £70 (full contour); £85 (veneered with e.max) | £138 |
| Zirconia implant crown | £125 (type not stated) | not listed | £330 (screw retained) |
| Porcelain bonded crown | £37-£80 (NHS to private) | from £65 | not listed |

Sources: [Impulse](https://www.impulsedentallab.co.uk/price-list/), [The Teeth People](https://www.theteethpeople.co.uk/dental-laboratory-uk-pricing/), [Elevate](https://elevatedental.co.uk/dental-lab-price-list/). [V]

- For similar-sounding items the highest list price is about 1.6x the lowest (e.max), 2x (zirconia, comparing £70 and £138) and 2.6x (zirconia implant crown). [I, my arithmetic on the table above]
- Impulse's own list varies by customer type: NHS, independent, private. So **what a practice actually pays depends on its tier and volume**, and list prices are not what practices pay. [V]
- **What this proves:** price differences between UK labs are large enough to be worth checking. **What it does not prove:** that a given practice overpays, or that quality is equal. That needs real invoices.
- Another source says lab bills commonly run 8-15% of turnover and that unregistered labs charge VAT that exempt practices cannot reclaim. [V, [Dental Finance Partners](https://www.dentalfinancepartners.co.uk/blog/practice-accounting/laboratory-costs-dental-accounts-uk)] VAT on lab invoices is a concrete thing the monthly check can catch. [I]
